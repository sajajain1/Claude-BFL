00 — Context

Sizing the problem before designing the solution

A 250,000-entity vendor population cannot be QA'd the way a 2,000-entity population is. The design has to start from throughput arithmetic, because that arithmetic decides the operating model — not the other way round.

The figures below are a planning model built from stated assumptions, not measured facts from your estate. Replace each with your actuals in week one; the shape of the conclusion is robust to fairly large changes in the inputs, but the FTE numbers are not.

That last figure is the actual problem. The department is not slow — it is blind across nineteen twentieths of the estate, and the sample it does review is chosen by availability rather than by risk. An AI QA layer's first contribution is not speed. It is coverage: a machine opinion on every assessment, which converts an unmeasured population into a measured one and lets the sampling become risk-directed.

Reviewer effort under this model is roughly 8,700 hours a year — about 5.5 FTE — against 145 FTE for equivalent manual coverage, while raising coverage from 5% to 100%. Add ~2 FTE for model oversight, back-testing and thematic analysis. The honest framing for your steering committee is not headcount reduction; it is a 20× increase in assurance coverage for roughly the team you have.

Do not skip this

These numbers depend entirely on the flag rate holding near 12% at acceptable precision. If the model flags 35% of assessments because your forms permit free-text everywhere, the reviewer queue becomes 16 FTE and the business case inverts. Form standardisation is a prerequisite, not a parallel workstream. Section 03 returns to this.

01 — Brief item 1

Target operating model

A federated model with a central AI assurance capability. The machine produces observations; only a human produces findings. That distinction is the load-bearing wall of the whole design, and every control in Section 08 rests on it.

Where the capability sits in the three lines

Structural rule

The team that builds the model must not be the team that disposes of its output, and neither may approve a change to the scoring weights. Weight changes are an ORMC decision with MRM sign-off, version-controlled and effective-dated — because a weight change silently re-tiers thousands of vendors and is, in substance, a change to risk appetite.

Four levels of machine autonomy

Autonomy is granted per assessment type and per vendor tier, and is earned through measured precision — never assumed at launch. Start every new check at L0 and promote only on evidence.

L0 — Shadow
Model scores everything; output visible only to the AI Assurance team. Used to measure precision against human decisions before anyone sees a flag. Minimum 6 weeks per new check.
L1 — Advisory
Observations shown to reviewers as suggestions with evidence citations. Reviewer decides independently. Override rate is the key metric.
L2 — Routing
Model decides who reviews what and in what order, and auto-accepts clean low-tier assessments. Blocked for Tier 1 vendors, which always get human eyes.
L3 — Auto-disposition
Model closes clean assessments in Tier 4/5 with 5% blind sampling. Never available for policy-breach observations, adverse findings, or any tier change.
Hard limits — no autonomy level lifts these

The system never: downgrades a vendor's risk tier without human approval; closes an adverse or breach observation autonomously; issues any communication to a vendor; terminates, suspends or blocks a vendor; or auto-accepts any Tier 1 (critical/material) assessment. Adverse outcomes are always human-decided, and always attributable to a named reviewer.

Reviewer workbench — the actual daily object

Most programmes of this kind fail at the reviewer's screen rather than in the model. If dispositioning a flag takes more than about ninety seconds of navigation, reviewers batch-approve and the assurance value evaporates. Design requirements:

  • One queue, risk-ordered. Not a folder of assessments — a stream of observations sorted by vendor tier × observation severity × ageing. The reviewer never chooses what to work on.
  • Evidence in the same frame. The flagged answer, the model's stated reason, the cited policy clause, and the attached evidence with the relevant span highlighted — visible together, no tab-switching.
  • Four keystroke dispositions. Accept observation / Reject with reason / Escalate / Request rework. Rejection reason is a mandatory picklist because it is the training signal.
  • Reviewer disagreement is data, not friction. Every override feeds weekly precision reporting and the retraining backlog.
  • Bulk actions with a hard cap. Reviewers may accept at most 20 like-for-like observations at once, and bulk actions are excluded from the blind-sample denominator so they cannot dilute the control.
02 — Brief item 2

AI and GenAI use cases

Sequenced by value over difficulty. Note the pattern column: roughly half of what you asked for is not a GenAI problem at all, and solving it with rules is cheaper, faster, explainable to a regulator, and 100% reproducible.

Design principle

Deterministic first, LLM only where language judgment is genuinely required. Missing-field detection is a rule. Evidence-expiry checking is a date comparison. Contradiction detection across a fixed question set is a rule matrix. Reserve the model — and the model-risk overhead that comes with it — for the three things only it can do: judging whether prose is substantive, extracting facts from unstructured evidence, and clustering findings by meaning.

The highest-leverage use case is upstream, not downstream

"Assessment-in-flight guidance" sits in Wave 3 above for sequencing reasons, but it deserves separate emphasis. Every observation the QA layer raises is a defect that already escaped into the record and now costs a review cycle, a rework request and a re-submission. A lightweight check at the point of completion — this justification cites no control owner or date; assessments like this are returned 60% of the time — prevents the defect instead of detecting it.

Expect in-flight guidance to reduce the downstream flag rate materially within two cycles. It also changes the political character of the programme: the AI becomes something that helps the 1LoD finish faster rather than something that marks their homework. That matters more than it sounds for a 250,000-vendor rollout depending on business-unit cooperation.

Justification sufficiency — the rubric, not the vibe

"Weak justification" must be operationalised or it becomes an inconsistent, unappealable model opinion. Score each free-text justification on five binary-to-ternary criteria, and have the model return the criteria — not a holistic score:

  1. Specificity — names a control, system, process or document, versus generic assurance language.
  2. Attribution — identifies an owner, team or accountable party.
  3. Currency — references a date, period or frequency within the assessment window.
  4. Evidence linkage — points to an attachment or artefact that exists in the submission.
  5. Responsiveness — actually answers the question asked, rather than an adjacent one.

The model outputs the five criteria with a one-line rationale and a verbatim quote for each. The score is computed from the criteria in code. This makes the output auditable, tunable without prompt changes, stable across model versions, and defensible to a reviewer who disagrees — you can point at which criterion failed and why.

Non-negotiable for regulated use

Every model-generated observation must carry: the question ID, a verbatim quote of the text it judged, the policy or regulatory clause ID it was judged against, the rubric criterion that failed, the prompt version, the model version, and the retrieval snapshot ID. An observation without a citation trail is not usable in a regulated QA process — it cannot be defended in an audit or contested by a business unit, and it should be discarded rather than shown.

Three data problems that will decide the outcome

1. Vendor identity resolution

A 250,000-row vendor master almost certainly contains substantial duplication — the same legal entity as separate records per business unit, per procurement system, per spelling. Every downstream number is wrong until this is fixed: concentration risk is understated, a critical vendor hides behind a low-tier duplicate, and thematic analysis fragments across aliases. Build an entity-resolution layer keyed on registration numbers, tax IDs and LEIs, with fuzzy-match candidates queued for human confirmation. Budget for this properly. It is unglamorous, it is typically the largest single line in a programme like this, and skipping it invalidates the analytics you are building.

2. The clause library is the actual product

Conformance checking is only as good as the corpus it retrieves from. You need internal policy and applicable regulation decomposed to individual testable obligations, each with a stable clause ID, effective-from and effective-to dates, applicability rules (which entity, which vendor tier, which jurisdiction), and a mapping to the assessment questions that evidence it. Regulation changes and re-scoring must be reproducible against the version in force on the assessment date — which means retrieval is date-aware, not just semantic. This is a compliance-authoring task, not an engineering one, and it needs named owners in your department.

3. Question standardisation gates everything

If the same control is asked six different ways across six assessment templates, you cannot detect contradictions, cluster themes, or compare vendors. Map every question in every template to a canonical control taxonomy — an internal library anchored to a recognised framework such as ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A, NIST CSF 2.0 or your own control catalogue. Retire and merge templates aggressively as part of Wave 1. This is the prerequisite flagged in Section 00, and it is where the flag rate is actually decided.

Security control — vendor-supplied documents are untrusted input

Evidence attachments come from outside the bank and are processed by a language model. Treat every uploaded document as hostile input: a PDF can contain instructions addressed to your model. Extracted document text must be passed as data, never concatenated into the instruction portion of a prompt; the model must have no tool access during evidence review; outputs must be schema-validated and constrained to the question set; and any attempt at instruction-like content in an attachment should itself raise an observation for human review. This is a real and current attack path against exactly this class of system.

04 — Brief item 4

Risk scoring methodology

Three separate scores, deliberately not merged. Conflating assessment quality with vendor risk is the most common failure in this design — a perfectly completed assessment can describe a dangerous vendor, and a badly completed one can describe a safe vendor whose owner was in a hurry.

AQS — Assessment Quality Score
0–100. Did this assessment meet documentation standards? A process-quality measure. Owned by 2LoD.
VCR — Vendor Composite Risk
0–100 residual risk, mapped to five tiers. Drives oversight intensity and assessment cadence.
EWI — Early Warning Index
A velocity measure over trailing windows. Detects deterioration between assessments, which is when it actually happens.
Deliberately absent
No single "vendor health score". Composite-of-composites hides the driver, and management will act on the number without asking what moved it.

Assessment Quality Score

AQS = 100 − Σ (deductioni × severity_weighti) clamped to [0,100] Dimension caps max deduction per dimension Completeness 25 mandatory fields, conditional logic, unjustified N/A Evidence integrity 20 presence, type match, validity window, period coverage Justification quality 20 five-criterion rubric, per Section 02 Internal consistency 15 contradiction matrix across answer pairs Policy conformance 15 mapped clause coverage, date-aware Data hygiene 5 duplicate text, completion-time anomaly, copy-forward Bands 85–100 Accept auto-close eligible below Tier 1, 5% blind sample 70–84 Advisory observations logged, no rework required 55–69 Rework returned to 1LoD with itemised observations 0–54 Reject assessment not fit for reliance; escalated

Blocking rules override the score

Some defects are disqualifying regardless of a high AQS. These bypass the bands entirely and route straight to human review: expired or out-of-scope evidence for a critical control; a stated regulatory non-compliance; an answer contradicting the executed contract; an unconsented sub-outsourcing arrangement; assessment completed by an unauthorised or self-interested party. A 92 AQS with expired SOC 2 coverage is not an acceptable assessment, and the arithmetic must not be allowed to say otherwise.

Worked example

Observations raised: 6. Reviewer disposition required within 5 business days.

The 0.6 cap on control mitigation is a deliberate conservatism: no amount of demonstrated control brings a critical, irreplaceable, cross-border vendor into the low-risk tier. Regulators expect inherent criticality to remain visible in the residual position, and a framework that lets excellent paperwork erase structural dependency will not survive supervisory review.

Confidence, not just score

Every VCR carries a confidence measure derived from data freshness, evidence completeness and source coverage. A vendor scored 34 on eight-month-old data with three missing inputs is a different management object from one scored 34 on fresh, complete data. Surface both. Low confidence on a low-risk vendor is itself an early-warning signal — it usually means nobody has looked, which is exactly the condition under which unpleasant surprises originate.

Early Warning Index

Deterioration is a rate, not a level. The EWI z-scores each signal against the vendor's own trailing baseline and its peer group, then fires on a two-of-eight rule to suppress single-signal noise.

Engagement decay deserves particular attention. Vendors heading for trouble typically go quiet before anything measurable appears in the incident data — missed assessment deadlines, unanswered evidence requests, and repeated changes of relationship contact are among the earliest available signals and cost nothing to collect, because you already have them.

Alert discipline

Set the early-warning threshold so the total alert volume is what your team can genuinely investigate — roughly 40–60 open alerts at any time for a team of this size, not 4,000. An unactionable alert queue is worse than no alert queue: it creates a documented record that the institution was told and did nothing, which is materially harder to defend to a supervisor than not having detected it at all.

Design rules for these surfaces

  • Always show the denominator. "47 exceptions" is meaningless; "47 of 210 assessments (22%)" is a decision.
  • Stamp the as-of time and confidence on every page. Committee members will otherwise treat a stale monthly view as live.
  • Every aggregate drills to the record. If a director cannot get from a red cell to the named vendor and the specific observation in two clicks, they will stop trusting the aggregate.
  • Distinguish machine observations from human findings visually and permanently. These are different objects with different evidentiary weight, and the distinction must survive into exports and committee packs.
  • Row-level security by business unit via Entra ID group membership — with a compliance role that sees the whole estate.
  • Trend before status. A vendor at moderate risk deteriorating for three quarters matters more than a static high-risk vendor that is already controlled and monitored.
  • Design the export. Board packs will be circulated as PDF regardless of your intentions — make the print layout deliberate rather than accidental.
06 — Brief item 6

Implementation roadmap

Sequenced so that the first ninety days produce a defensible go/no-go decision on real data, not a proof of concept that impresses a steering committee and then dies on contact with the vendor master.

Days 1–30 — Establish ground truth

  • Measure the baseline. Take 400 historical assessments, have two senior reviewers independently QA them, and record every defect found. This is your golden dataset and your only objective measure of whether the model works. Nothing else in the programme substitutes for it.
  • Measure human agreement first. Where the two reviewers disagree, the standard is ambiguous — and no model can beat an ambiguous standard. Expect 15–25% disagreement, and resolve those cases into written rules before building anything.
  • Profile the vendor master: duplication rate, tier distribution, completeness, orphan records. Publish the number honestly; it sets programme expectations.
  • Inventory assessment templates and map questions to a canonical control taxonomy. Identify the merge and retire list.
  • Stand up the Fabric workspace, landing zone, Entra groups and Purview catalog. Ingest 90 days of assessments to bronze.
  • Register the initiative with Model Risk Management and start the AI-use-case governance intake now — not after build. In most institutions this is the longest-lead item on the plan.
  • Deliverable: baseline defect rates, inter-reviewer agreement, data quality report, approved scope.

Days 31–60 — Deterministic engine in shadow

  • Build and version the rules library: completeness, conditional logic, evidence validity, contradiction matrix, data hygiene. Target 150–200 rules covering the top defect categories from the baseline.
  • Run in shadow mode over the 90-day backlog. Compare against the golden dataset. Report precision and recall per rule, and retire rules below 70% precision rather than defending them.
  • Build the reviewer workbench in Power Apps against real observations. Two reviewers use it daily; iterate on their friction, not on your assumptions about it.
  • Prototype the AQS with ORMC-provisional weights. Publish the distribution across the backlog — if 90% of assessments score above 85, the model is not discriminating and the weights are wrong.
  • Assemble the clause library for one regulation and one internal policy end-to-end as a pattern proof.
  • Deliverable: shadow-mode precision report, working workbench, AQS distribution, go/no-go on the GenAI wave.

Days 61–90 — GenAI layer, one segment live

  • Add justification-sufficiency scoring with the five-criterion rubric. Validate against the golden dataset before it reaches any reviewer.
  • Add document intelligence for the three highest-volume evidence types — most likely SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 certificates and BCP test reports.
  • Implement the full decision record: prompt version, model version, retrieval snapshot, citations, reviewer, disposition, timestamp — into WORM storage.
  • Go live at L1 (advisory) for one business unit and one assessment type. Not the whole estate. Pick a segment with clean data and a cooperative owner; you are proving the operating model, not the scale.
  • MRM validation pack submitted. Executive dashboard v1 published to ORMC.
  • Deliverable: production advisory QA on a live segment, validated model, first thematic analysis, measured reviewer effort per disposition.

Months 4–12 — Scale and earn autonomy

  • Roll out by business unit, one at a time, each with its own baseline and its own precision gate. Resist the pressure to enable everything at once.
  • Promote checks from L1 to L2 individually as each demonstrates ≥85% precision over 8 weeks. Some checks will never be promoted; that is a correct outcome, not a failure.
  • Introduce L3 auto-disposition for Tier 4/5 only, with 5% blind sampling and monthly escape-rate reporting to ORMC.
  • Ship thematic clustering and the early-warning engine. Run EWI in shadow for a full quarter before alerts reach anyone.
  • Deploy in-flight guidance to 1LoD preparers — the upstream intervention from Section 02, and the largest single lever on flag rate.
  • First annual weight recalibration and back-test against realised incidents.

Months 12–24 — Continuous assurance

The destination is not faster periodic assessment. It is the retirement of periodic assessment as the primary control for most of the population.

  • Risk-adaptive cadence. Assessment frequency driven by VCR and EWI rather than by the calendar. Stable low-risk vendors move to continuous signal monitoring with attestation only; deteriorating vendors are assessed on trigger. This is where the 250,000-vendor population becomes genuinely manageable.
  • Nth-party graph across the estate, with concentration and single-point-of-failure analysis at the function level rather than the vendor level.
  • Scenario capability: model the operational impact of a named critical provider failing, per the impact-tolerance expectations now common across major regimes.
  • Regulator-ready register generation directly from the lakehouse, versioned and reproducible as at any past date.
  • Portfolio-level predictive view: which vendor characteristics precede findings, feeding back into onboarding due diligence.

This distinction is worth getting explicitly right before anyone demonstrates a chat interface to your committee.

Microsoft 365 Copilot — reviewer productivity
Summarising a long vendor history, drafting committee narrative, querying policy documents. Appropriate, low-risk, immediately useful.
Copilot Studio — a governed Q&A agent
"What does our policy require for sub-outsourcing consent?" grounded on the approved clause library, published to Teams with citations. Appropriate.
Copilot as the QA control itself
No. A control must be deterministic in invocation, versioned, reproducible and independently testable. An ad hoc chat session is none of those things.
The rule to hold
Copilot helps a human think. The pipeline performs the control. Anything that produces a score, an observation or a routing decision runs in the orchestrated pipeline with a full decision record — never in a chat window.

Cost shape to plan against

At roughly 310,000 assessments a year with an average of perhaps 40 free-text answers each, the generative workload is real but bounded — and it is dominated by input tokens from retrieved clauses and extracted evidence, not by output. Three levers do most of the work: run deterministic checks first and only invoke the model on answers that need language judgment (typically a third); batch overnight on provisioned throughput rather than interactively; and cache aggressively on the clause corpus, which changes rarely. Model the cost per assessment explicitly and report it monthly as a KPI — it is the number that determines whether the platform scales to the full population or stalls at Wave 2.

08 — Brief item 8

Compliance, governance and human-review controls

The supervisory question will not be "does your AI work?" It will be "how do you know, who checked, what happens when it is wrong, and can you show me a decision from eighteen months ago exactly as it was made?" Design for that question from day one; it is very hard to retrofit.

The recursive point people miss

If this platform runs on cloud infrastructure and uses third-party model services, the QA system is itself an outsourcing arrangement and must be assessed under your own framework — quite possibly as a material one, since it supports the outsourcing control function. Run it through your own process, including exit strategy, concentration analysis and audit rights over the provider. Failing to do so is a finding that writes itself, and it is exactly the kind of thing a supervisor enjoys discovering.

Human review controls

  1. Machine output is an observation; only a human creates a finding. The data model must enforce this — observations and findings are separate entities with separate lifecycles, and no automated path converts one into the other.
  2. Named accountability. Every disposition carries a named reviewer. "The system decided" is never an acceptable answer to a supervisor or a business unit.
  3. Four-eyes on consequential outcomes: tier changes, critical-vendor findings, escalations to committee, and any first-of-kind observation type.
  4. Mandatory human review for every Tier 1 vendor assessment, every policy-breach observation, every early-warning critical alert, and every case where model confidence is below threshold.
  5. Blind sampling as a control on the model: 5% of auto-accepted assessments re-reviewed by humans who cannot see the machine score. Escape rate reported monthly to ORMC.
  6. Override rights are unconditional. Any reviewer may reject any observation with a reason code, without approval. A system reviewers cannot overrule is a system they will learn to route around.
  7. Appeal path for business units. A vendor owner who disputes an observation gets independent human re-review within a defined SLA. Without this, the programme accumulates political resistance faster than it accumulates coverage.
  8. Automation-bias monitoring. Track per-reviewer acceptance rates and time-per-disposition. A reviewer accepting 99% of observations at eight seconds each is not reviewing, and that is a control failure to manage rather than a productivity result to celebrate.

Audit trail — the reproducibility standard

Every observation must be reconstructable years later. The immutable record captures:

assessment_id · question_id · verbatim_input_hash model_id · model_version · deployment_region prompt_template_id · prompt_version retrieval_snapshot_id · clause_ids_returned · corpus_effective_date rules_library_version · scoring_weights_version · thresholds_version raw_model_output · parsed_criteria · confidence observation_id · severity · citation_spans reviewer_id · disposition · reason_code · timestamp · elapsed subsequent_appeal · outcome

Held in WORM storage with a retention period set by your records schedule — commonly 7–10 years for outsourcing records, and it should outlive the vendor relationship plus the limitation period. The test to run in year one: pick a closed observation from six months ago and reproduce its exact inputs, retrieved clauses and output. If you cannot, the audit trail is decorative, and you will discover this during an inspection rather than during a rehearsal.

Model governance and ongoing monitoring

Pre-deployment
Golden-dataset validation, documented precision/recall per check, independent MRM review, documented known limitations and failure modes, ORMC approval of thresholds.
Change control
Prompt, model version, rule and weight changes are all controlled changes. Regression against the golden dataset before release. No silent model upgrades — pin versions and test provider updates explicitly.
Drift monitoring
Weekly precision, flag rate, score distribution and override rate by segment. Alarm on statistically significant shifts, and treat an unexplained flag-rate change as an incident.
Fairness testing
Flag rates compared across vendor size, geography, language and business unit. A model systematically penalising small or non-English-speaking vendors for prose quality rather than control quality is a real and likely failure mode here — test for it deliberately, quarterly.
Annual revalidation
Full independent revalidation, weight back-testing against realised incidents and findings, and a documented challenger comparison.
Kill switch
A tested, documented procedure to revert to L0 shadow or full manual QA, with defined trigger conditions and a named decision-maker. Rehearse it once a year.

Data protection

  • Vendor assessments contain limited personal data — contacts, signatories, occasionally individual audit findings. Minimise at ingest; do not send personal data to the model where a token or role reference will do.
  • Enforce data residency for model inference in line with your regulatory obligations; document the location of every processing step, including the retrieval index.
  • Contractually exclude your data from provider model training, and evidence that exclusion in the platform's own vendor assessment.
  • Apply Purview sensitivity labels at ingest and let them flow through to Power BI exports, which is where confidential vendor data most often escapes.

If you do only one thing first

Build the golden dataset. Four hundred assessments, two independent senior reviewers, every defect recorded and every disagreement resolved into a written rule. It costs a few reviewer-weeks, it requires no technology decision, no procurement and no committee approval — and without it you have no way to tell whether any of the rest of this works. Every subsequent decision in this document, from rule retirement to autonomy promotion to MRM validation, resolves against that dataset.
